Review

Project Hail Mary opens on a man waking up in a bed on a spaceship with no memory of his own name, and it never really lets go of that disorientation, in the best way. Ryan Gosling plays Ryland Grace, a middle school science teacher and former molecular biologist who slowly pieces together that he is the last surviving member of a crew sent nearly twelve light years from Earth to stop a microbial threat that is dimming the sun. The film cuts between his present, isolated struggle to survive and flashbacks that unpack how an ordinary teacher ended up humanity's last option.

What surprised me most is how much of the movie's heart comes from a relationship that starts as pure science-fiction novelty and turns into the emotional center of the film. Without spoiling the specifics, the back half leans hard into themes of friendship, sacrifice, and problem-solving under pressure, and it earns every bit of the payoff. Lord and Miller, better known for comedy, bring a surprisingly steady hand to the quieter, more procedural stretches, letting the science feel tactile instead of hand-wavy.

"It's a survival story that keeps finding new things to survive, and a friendship story that sneaks up on you."

Pros & Cons

What Works

  • Gosling carries long stretches of solo screen time convincingly
  • The central alien relationship is genuinely affecting
  • Science-heavy problem solving stays tense instead of dry
  • Strong score that knows when to go quiet

What Doesn't

  • Flashback structure slows the middle stretch
  • Some side characters get little room to breathe
  • A few plot conveniences late in the film
